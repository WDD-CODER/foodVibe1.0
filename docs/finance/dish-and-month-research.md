# Research sharpens FoodVibe's dish and month plans

The two FoodVibe feasibility reports hold up well against how restaurant back-office tools work. Of their 17 decisions, **11 stay as they are, 4 stay with a refinement, and 2 change**. Neither change is to architecture. Dish decision 4 (dishes without a recipe) and monthly decision 10 (build order) change because both leave out something every vendor treats as core. In dish analysis, sales arrive as a product-mix file mapped once to recipes, and uncosted items are handled rather than dropped. In monthly planning, labour is needed for prime cost, the headline number, and fixed lines are copied forward from the first month. The research also adds **19 decisions the reports do not ask**. The ones that change the numbers most are these: classify dishes within their category, and say which thresholds are used. Compute margin on prices net of 18% VAT and net of discounts, and leave tips out. Give each sales name a portion factor so sizes cost correctly. Keep "not entered" apart from zero in every month. Turn gross salary into employer cost with a burden factor. Compute labour totals on the server, so a role that sees totals only never receives per-person rows. Two facts drive the proposed slice changes. In reviews, the main complaint is setup effort: mapping and recipe building, not the matrix itself. And for an Israeli small business the cheapest privacy design is a monthly labour total, not per-employee payroll. So the dish track pulls file import forward and folds uncosted items into the menu slice. The month track pulls labour totals, invoice food cost and carry-forward ahead of AI capture, and defers payslip capture indefinitely. One thing still blocks a confident import design: no public documentation shows what Tabit, Beecomm or Green Invoice actually export. Get one real file from a target owner before the parser is built.

## Every dish report starts with a mapped sales file

All the products checked take dish counts from a point-of-sale product-mix (PMIX) report. They need a one-time mapping from each POS button, modifier and size to a recipe before any dish report works. MarginEdge tells customers they must "map your product mix" first ([MarginEdge help](https://help.marginedge.com/hc/en-us/articles/360038991914)). Toast's xtraCHEF counts mapping as complete only when **every active menu item and modifier is mapped**, and its PMIX and actual-vs-theoretical reports stay empty until then ([Toast support](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/xtraCHEF-Recipe-Product-Mix-Mapping)). Mapping is hard enough that MarginEdge sells it as a paid setup service for roughly **$250–$1,250** ([MarginEdge theoretical setup](https://www.marginedge.com/professional-services/theoretical-setup)). Restaurant365 reviewers name setup difficulty as their top complaint, with about **4 months** to implement ([G2 Restaurant365](https://www.g2.com/products/restaurant365/reviews)). For an app with no POS integration, meez is the closest model: it imports a CSV or Excel POS sales report, matches rows by item name, and creates menu items for names it does not know ([meez blog](https://getmeez.com/blog/menu-building-costing-and-engineering)).

This weakens dish decision 2 more than any other finding. The feasibility report recommends typing sales by hand first because it needs no parser. That is true, and a typed form is still needed for very small menus and for return counts. But the operator research concludes that hand-typing beyond about 20–30 items is risky. A month-end routine survives only while it stays around five minutes, and the obstacle is discipline more than time ([G2 discussion](https://www.g2.com/discussions/how-do-you-keep-month-end-data-entry-disciplined-in-chef-cost-lab-or-similar-tools)). The 20–30-item figure is the researchers' inference, not a measured threshold. Typing should stay as the fallback, and the file import with remembered aliases should move up to sit right behind the analysis function. FoodVibe already has the alias pattern for invoices, so the matching idea is proven in-house.

The mapping table must also carry a **portion factor**, not just a target dish. xtraCHEF maps each size as a different quantity of the recipe's serving size ([Toast support](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/xtraCHEF-Recipe-Product-Mix-Mapping)). MarginEdge tells Toast users to "map the version that includes the size in the item name" ([MarginEdge Toast PMIX](https://help.marginedge.com/hc/en-us/articles/40463692991123-Toast-PMIX-Enhancements)). The feasibility report's answer to "two sizes of one dish" is to sum many names into one item. That gets the units right, but a large shakshuka at 1.5 portions would be costed as one. Modifiers are the other silent leak: unmapped modifiers "will not be included in either cost or the revenue" ([MarginEdge help](https://help.marginedge.com/hc/en-us/articles/360015329433)), and an unmapped item posts a zero-cost sale that resurfaces later as unexplained variance ([Supy](https://supy.io/blog/learn-pos-item-ingredient-mapping-standardization)). The report's plan to block the apply until every unmatched name is matched, skipped or added matches xtraCHEF's definition of "complete". It should stay.

The Israeli import path is the biggest unknown. No public documentation was found on whether Tabit or Beecomm export item sales as CSV or Excel. Tabit's back-office help shows a Reports menu but says nothing about item export ([Tabit support](https://support-us.tabit.cloud/hc/en-us/articles/32265793525650-Offers-report)). One concrete route does exist for small businesses that invoice through Green Invoice (Morning) rather than a restaurant POS: its "items sold" report, built from saved items ([Green Invoice](https://www.greeninvoice.co.il/help-center/items-report/)). The import slice should not start until one real export from a target owner is in hand.

## The matrix needs categories, net prices and an honest place for uncosted dishes

The textbook Kasavana–Smith rule calls a dish popular when its share of units exceeds **70% of an equal share** (0.7 × 1/N, so 7% on a 10-dish menu). It calls a dish profitable when its contribution margin (CM) beats the menu's average. The rule is applied within a comparable group of dishes ([meez](https://www.getmeez.com/blog/menu-engineering-matrix), [FIU Hospitality Review](https://digitalcommons.fiu.edu/record/17973/files/V13n195e.pdf)). Commercial tools simplify this in different ways. Restaurant365 uses plain averages of sales amount and margin, and renames Plowhorse "Opportunity". Because its averages span whatever items the filters leave in, a filter change can move a dish between quadrants ([R365 docs](https://docs.restaurant365.com/docs/menu-item-analysis)). Oracle Simphony compares each item's percentage of quantity and of margin against the average ([Oracle docs](https://docs.oracle.com/en/industries/food-beverage/reporting-and-analytics/rarrg/c_menu_eng_add.htm)). Even the CM average differs by source: meez uses a simple average of item CMs, while the academic version weights by units sold. The feasibility report already chose the 0.7 rule and a weighted average, which is the defensible textbook pair. What it lacks is **classification per category**. A matrix that mixes espresso and steak puts every coffee in "Plowhorse" and every steak in "Puzzle". R365's category filters, Oracle's group rows and MarginEdge's within-category comparison all exist to prevent that ([MarginEdge](https://www.marginedge.com/menu-analysis)). FoodVibe can classify within `course` and state the thresholds on screen. If a course has too few dishes for a stable average, it should fall back to the whole menu. The minimum count for that fallback is a design choice, not a sourced number.

VAT matters more in Israel than in the US tools. Guidance from VAT countries is to compute gross profit on prices net of VAT, because VAT-inclusive prices overstate margins ([Pulse Accountants](https://pulse-accountants.co.uk/blog/menu-pricing-gross-profit-hospitality), [Kitchen Numbers](https://kitchennmbrs.app/en/knowledge-base/basic-knowledge-and-formulas/how-do-i-process-vat-in-my-selling-prices-as-a-food-service-entrepreneur)). Oracle's report states it is built for add-on (US-style) taxes. Israel's rate has been **18% since 1 January 2025** ([Herzog](https://herzoglaw.co.il/en/news-and-insights/vat-updates-for-2025-vat-rate-increase-israel-invoices-reform-and-the-economic-plan-bill/)). Mixing a VAT-inclusive menu price with a VAT-exclusive supplier cost shows a ₪30 plate on a ₪118 menu price at 25.4% food cost instead of the true 30%. Discounts distort the picture the same way. A MarginEdge customer who loves its menu analysis still abandoned it for pricing decisions because it runs on gross rather than net sales, which their discount program skews ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/187718/MarginEdge/reviews/)). Oracle measures margin on sales less item discounts. Tips should stay out of dish revenue. The Israel Tax Authority now treats waiters' tips as subject to VAT for transactions from 31 March 2025, which makes them easy to mix into revenue by mistake ([Afik](https://www.afiklaw.com/updates/17194)). FoodVibe's existing `buildMarginReport` already divides by (1 + VAT), so the rule carries over. The open question is where the rate lives, and both reports should use the same place.

Uncosted dishes are where the feasibility report is weakest. It recommends leaving dishes without a recipe out of version one. The vendors do the opposite. R365 lets the owner type an "Estimated Cost" on the menu item, and MarginEdge and xtraCHEF let a POS button map directly to a purchased product instead of a recipe ([R365 docs](https://docs.restaurant365.com/docs/menu-item-analysis), [Toast support](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/xtraCHEF-Recipe-Product-Mix-Mapping)). Where neither is done, R365's default "Recipe Cost > 0" filter removes the item from the matrix ([R365 dashboard](https://docs.restaurant365.com/docs/r365-intelligence-menu-engineering-dashboard-master)). For a café, bought-in drinks and desserts can be a large share of units. Leaving them out distorts the menu-mix denominator and loses revenue from every total. FoodVibe already holds products with current prices, so "link to a product" costs almost nothing beyond the typed-cost field. The better version-one rule gives every sold item one of three states: costed by recipe; costed by a linked product or a typed cost; or units-only. Units-only items count in the sales mix but are excluded from the CM averages and labelled. Dishes with a partial recipe cost get the same treatment as units-only, which the report's D8 row already hints at.

On frozen versus live cost, the vendors split. Most (R365, MarketMan, Supy) re-cost with current ingredient prices, and MarginEdge averages each day's ingredient cost over the report range ([MarginEdge help](https://help.marginedge.com/hc/en-us/articles/360038991914)). Neither design suits a monthly review whose past months must stop moving. The report's choice to store the cost per portion with its partial flag is right. One refinement: keep the cost live while the month is open, and freeze it when the month is closed. Then the dish freeze and the monthly lock become one event.

Returns follow a clear industry pattern. Square makes a reason mandatory for every comp or void, chosen from an admin-defined list ([Square help](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/5814-get-started-with-comp-and-void)). Toast reports voids by reason, server and approver ([Toast](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Exceptions-Report-Overview)). This confirms dish decision 3: a fixed list with "other", plus an optional note. Back-office menu engineering rarely folds returns into the matrix. The one exception is R365's "Priority" column, which adds the Waste Log to the cost sold. That is a precedent for showing return cost as its own figure next to the dish without changing its class. Labour should stay out of dish CM altogether: none of R365, Oracle or meez includes a labour field in menu-engineering margin ([meez](https://www.getmeez.com/blog/menu-engineering-matrix)). That makes dish slice 8 (labour roll-up) optional and last. The insight text can stay rule-based. R365 ships one static action per quadrant ("consider removing next quarter" for Dogs) ([R365 dashboard](https://docs.restaurant365.com/docs/r365-intelligence-menu-engineering-dashboard-master)). The actions owners take most are removing items and raising prices: in an NRA survey cited secondhand, **53%** of operators said they removed menu items to cope with cost increases ([NetSuite](https://www.netsuite.com/portal/resource/articles/business-strategy/menu-engineering-your-way-to-restaurant-profitability.shtml)). That favours a "margin slipped since last month" alert over a richer matrix. meez recommends a **30–90 day** sales window ([meez](https://www.getmeez.com/blog/menu-engineering-matrix)). A small café's single month is noisy, so the matrix should be able to combine up to three months.

## A monthly P&L rests on prime cost and honest blanks

Restaurant P&L layouts follow one order: sales; then cost of goods (COGS); then labour; then **prime cost** (COGS plus labour); then controllable expenses; then occupancy; then net profit. Prime cost is the headline. Targets cluster at 55–65% of sales: under 65% for full service and about 60% for quick service ([Toast](https://pos.toasttab.com/blog/on-the-line/prime-cost), [7shifts](https://7shifts.com/blog/restaurant-prime-cost-guide/)). Labour in prime cost must include payroll taxes and benefits, or prime cost is understated ([Toast](https://pos.toasttab.com/blog/on-the-line/restaurant-prime-cost), [MarketMan](https://www.marketman.com/blog/restaurant-prime-cost)). The Israeli bands are close. Consultants quoted in Maariv (9 July 2026) put cost of goods at about 30–35%, labour at 30–35%, and rent, arnona, utilities, insurance, kashrut and marketing at 20–25%. Net profit runs about **5–10%** even for a stable restaurant ([Maariv](https://www.maariv.co.il/economy/israel/article-1342297)). Two findings follow for the monthly report. First, its slice order shows profit and "room for the team" before any labour input exists, and prime cost needs labour. Second, a month needs more targets than one profit %: at least food %, labour %, prime % and profit %, seeded from the Israeli bands and editable. A kashrut line is worth adding to the starter list of fixed lines.

Labour needs both a burden factor and a privacy design. Employer cost in Israel runs well above gross pay. Employer National Insurance is **4.51% up to ₪7,703 a month and 7.6% above**, a temporary rate valid to the end of 2026 ([Bituach Leumi](https://www.btl.gov.il/Insurance/Rates/Pages/zmani.aspx)). On top of that come pension at 6.5% and severance at 6–8.33%. Commercial calculators put the total uplift at about **+15–31%** depending on assumptions ([JobCalc](https://jobcalc.co.il/salary/employer-cost/minimum-wage/), [Olam HaAvoda](https://olam-haavoda.co.il/EmployerCostCalculator)). An owner who types gross salaries without a factor understates prime cost by a sixth or more. The simplest design takes a typed employer-cost total from the payroll provider or accountant. When the owner knows only gross pay, the app multiplies it by an editable factor (default about 1.25) and labels the result an estimate. On privacy, 7shifts is the warning case. It gates wages behind a "Can view wages" permission, yet documents that managers with report permissions can still see wage data on reports ([7shifts](https://kb.7shifts.com/hc/en-us/articles/33060597629715-Manager-access-to-wage-data)). The lesson is that every aggregate must be computed on the server, so a totals-only role never receives per-person rows. Israel's Privacy Protection Law Amendment 13 has applied since 14 August 2025, and payroll specialists say SMBs handling payroll data are "generally bound" by its purpose, security and access duties ([Malam Payroll](https://www.malam-payroll.com/%D7%AA%D7%99%D7%A7%D7%95%D7%9F-13-%D7%9C%D7%97%D7%95%D7%A7-%D7%94%D7%92%D7%A0%D7%AA-%D7%94%D7%A4%D7%A8%D7%98%D7%99%D7%95%D7%AA-%D7%9E%D7%95%D7%A8%D7%94-%D7%A0%D7%91%D7%95%D7%9B%D7%99%D7%9D/), [Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/item/global-legal-monitor/2025-11-17/israel-amendment-to-privacy-protection-law-goes-into-effect/)). Sources disagree on whether salary still counts as "special sensitivity" data after the definition was narrowed ([Goldfarb](https://www.goldfarb.com/amendment-13-to-the-israeli-privacy-protection-law/)), and that needs a lawyer. Either way, a monthly labour total carries almost none of the exposure that per-employee lines and payslip photos carry. That is why per-employee lines move later, and payslip capture is deferred until an owner asks for it.

This creates a new consultant question that touches INV-1. Today a consultant with a live grant may do whatever the owner may, except manage the team and change settings. If per-employee pay lines exist, the recommended rule shows the consultant labour totals only and keeps person-level rows owner-only. That makes the consultant rule narrower than INV-1 states, so it must go to the Human in the INV format, not be decided in a plan.

Missing data is where incumbents fail quietly. R365 fills a budget with zeros when prior-year data does not exist ([R365](https://docs.restaurant365.com/docs/creating-a-new-budget)). 7shifts leaves out staff without a wage and warns that "totals will be understated until all wages are complete" ([7shifts](https://kb.7shifts.com/hc/en-us/articles/52542834160659-Control-labor-costs-with-the-Labor-Budget-Tool-Getting-Started-for-Managers)). The monthly report already names "a month with no data counted as zero" as its biggest risk. The research turns that into a storage rule: every line keeps "not entered" (null) apart from an entered 0, and the month shows a completeness badge such as "3 of 9 lines entered". Profit and break-even are labelled "partial" until the key lines exist. Rough mode has real-world support: one spreadsheet-first operator tracks all operating expenses as one category day to day and splits them only at month-end ([Substack](https://yukafromcassavasf.substack.com/p/restaurant-finance-system-for-operators?r=mwvg3)).

## Purchases stand in for usage until someone counts stock

Every tool computes usage as opening stock plus purchases minus closing stock. Without counts in the period, MarginEdge's Controllable P&L sets COGS equal to purchases ([MarginEdge help](https://help.marginedge.com/hc/en-us/articles/39122493419923); read from a search snippet only). That confirms monthly decision 6 exactly: approved invoices dated in the month, a visible "purchases, not usage" label, and a stock correction later. Two invoice details from Supy belong in the build. First, finance locks a period's invoices in one pass at month-end. Second, header-level discounts are spread across lines in proportion to value before the lock: a 10% discount on a $1,950 beef line makes it $1,755. Otherwise unit costs and COGS are overstated ([Supy](https://supy.io/blog/learn-restaurant-month-end-close-lock-invoices)). No vendor documents a cutoff rule for a delivery in one month invoiced in the next. Using the tax-invoice date, and flagging invoices with no date rather than falling back to the day they were saved, keeps FoodVibe in line with the accountant's books. That is an inference, not a sourced rule. VAT on costs needs a setting for the dealer type. An עוסק מורשה reclaims input VAT, so costs are net. An עוסק פטור cannot, so its costs are gross. This also comes from the research team's inference rather than a cited source, and should be confirmed with an accountant.

Period handling has a mature pattern. R365 starts a budget blank, from prior-year actuals plus or minus a %, or as a copy of another budget. It locks a period with a closed date that is moved back to reopen, and its audit log records create, update, delete and approve ([R365 budgets](https://docs.restaurant365.com/docs/creating-a-new-budget), [R365 close](https://restaurant365-academy-com.s3.amazonaws.com/close-period.html), [R365 audit log](https://docs.restaurant365.com/docs/event-audit-log)). Supy corrects a locked invoice by unposting, fixing and reposting, which keeps the original cost for audit. It warns that deleting and re-keying breaks the trail. For FoodVibe that means a month-level Open → Closed status, reopening only with a typed reason, and a log of who, when, field, old value and new value. It also means a "start next month" action that copies fixed lines and targets, with an optional ±% uplift. Unlike R365, missing prior values should come across blank, not zero. Copy-forward is cheap and is the main convenience a monthly routine offers, so it belongs early, not as the eleventh slice. The monthly report's M9 asks for an approval step on each changed actual. The research supports a coarser and simpler rule: a whole month is open or closed, and a closed month can only be changed by reopening it.

Break-even sales are fixed costs ÷ (1 − variable costs ÷ sales). With $4,000 fixed costs and variable costs at 30%, break-even is **$5,714.29** a month ([Toast](https://pos.toasttab.com/blog/on-the-line/restaurant-costs-breakdown)). R365's weekly chart shows projected against actual sales, and labour spent against budget with "money left to spend for the week" ([R365](https://docs.restaurant365.com/docs/sales-and-labor-budget-chart)). The point of a weekly number is to act "while the period is still open" ([Ramp](https://ramp.com/blog/accounting-software-for-restaurants)). No vendor documents how a monthly target is split into days. The simple version is the monthly target divided by the month's open days, with the open-day count stored per month to handle Shabbat closures and holidays. Weekday weighting can come later.

Overhead allocation is where monthly decision 5 needs rewording more than reversing. Most tools price a dish as plate cost ÷ target food-cost %. A $5.00 plate at 30% gives $16.67 ([Toast](https://pos.toasttab.com/blog/on-the-line/restaurant-menu-pricing)). Overhead is managed on the P&L, not baked into each dish. Where overhead is allocated, it is total period overhead ÷ units sold ([Escoffier](https://www.escoffier.edu/?p=30115)) or a fixed profit per plate ([Petpooja](https://blog.petpooja.com/free-tools/menu-pricing-calculator-free/)). The report's "percent of the dish price" is the same maths as the industry method: when the target food-cost % equals 100 − labour % − overhead % − profit %, price = cost ÷ target food-cost %. So the recommendation stands, framed in the industry's terms. Compute the price net, and show it gross (× 1.18). An "overhead per dish sold" figure can appear once dish sales exist, but only as information.

## What the evidence cannot yet settle

Four gaps limit the conclusions. First, Israeli POS export formats (Tabit, Beecomm, Caspit) are undocumented online, so the import parser's column mapping is a guess until a real file arrives. Second, it is unresolved whether salary data counts as "special sensitivity" data under Amendment 13. Third, no source was found for a beverage-cost target, so it stays a typed, editable value. Fourth, no Reddit threads or interviews with small Israeli owners were reached; the operator voice comes from G2 and Capterra snippets and vendor help docs. Several MarginEdge help pages and the FIU and PSU textbooks were readable only through search snippets, which is noted where they are cited.

## Conclusion

The research shifts where the risk sits. The formulas in both reports are standard and low-risk. The numbers go wrong at the inputs: unmapped names, sizes counted as one portion, VAT-inclusive prices, gross pay instead of employer cost, and blanks stored as zeros. That argues for spending build effort on the intake and data-quality layer (aliases with portion factors, an unmapped queue, completeness badges, a single VAT and dealer-type setting) before the polish (insight sentences, labour roll-ups, AI bill capture). The reviews point the same way: operators praise accurate recipe cost and complain about setup, not about the matrix.

The two screens should also share three seams instead of duplicating them. Both should read one business-level VAT rate and dealer type. One month close should freeze dish costs and lock monthly actuals together. And one sales import should feed both the dish table and the month's revenue hint, so the owner enters a month's sales once.
